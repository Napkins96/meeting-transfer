    How it works

    Speech to text. Your browser's built-in speech recognizer types the words. Chrome sends the audio to Google's speech service to do this; Safari on a Mac can run it on the device.

    Pause detection. The page measures loudness about 23 times a second and learns the room's background level. Sound well above that level counts as speech; quiet longer than your pause setting starts a new paragraph.

    Speaker changes. For each stretch of speech it builds a small voiceprint: the shape of the voice's spectrum (MFCCs, the same features pro tools start from) plus the speaker's pitch. A stretch that doesn't match any known voiceprint starts a new speaker. When you stop, it groups every stretch again with the full recording in view, which is more accurate than the live guess.

    Videos and audio files. The page pulls the sound out of the file, finds the stretches of speech, and runs Whisper, an open-source speech model, inside your browser on each stretch. The model downloads once and is then kept by the browser. The file and its audio never leave your computer.

    Takeaways. The summary picks the lines that carry the meeting's most repeated subjects, then pulls out commitments, decisions and questions by their wording. For a summary Claude writes itself, press Summarize with Claude: it copies the transcript and opens Meeting Summarizer, where you paste it.
